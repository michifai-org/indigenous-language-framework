# Protocols

This project exists to serve Indigenous communities. How it handles language data matters more than any feature.

## The rule

**No community-specific language content belongs in this public repository.**

That means no vocabulary lists, sentences, stories, audio, video, transcriptions, grammar notes, photographs, names of speakers, or cultural knowledge belonging to any community. This repository contains the framework only: documents, templates, schemas, scripts and a demo in a clearly fictional language.

If community material is submitted here by mistake, it will be removed, and the contributor will be asked to move it to a private location.

## How communities should use the framework

1. Use GitHub's "Use this template" to create your own repository.
2. Set that repository to **private** before adding any content.
3. Decide who in your community has access, and keep that list small and deliberate.
4. Keep audio and other large or sensitive files in storage your community controls, rather than assuming GitHub is the right place for them.

Before relying on GitHub for anything sensitive, check its current terms and privacy practices for yourselves. Hosting on any third-party platform means trusting that platform to some degree. Some communities will reasonably decide not to host their material on GitHub at all. The toolkit is designed so that its scripts can run entirely on a local computer.

## Guiding frameworks

These are widely used in Indigenous data governance. They are listed so users can find them, not as a claim that this project satisfies them:

- **OCAP®** (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession), developed by the First Nations Information Governance Centre, with a focus on First Nations in Canada.
- **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance** (Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics), developed by the Global Indigenous Data Alliance.

Many nations, including Métis governments and organizations, have their own protocols, policies or research agreements. **Where a community has its own protocols, they take precedence over anything in this document.** Please learn what yours are before starting.

## Questions to settle before adding material

Before adding any recording, text or story to a community copy of the toolkit, a program should be able to answer:

- **Who owns this material?** Is it held by the community, a family, an individual speaker, an archive, a university or a government body?
- **Do we have permission to use it for teaching?** This includes archival and historical recordings, which may carry restrictions that are not obvious.
- **Did the speaker, or their family if the speaker has passed, consent to this use?** Consent for one purpose is not consent for every purpose.
- **Is any of it restricted?** Some knowledge, stories, songs and names are not meant for general sharing, or only for certain people or seasons.
- **Who may see it?** Learners, the whole community, or only the program?
- **What happens if someone withdraws consent?** There should be a way to remove their material.
- **Does it involve children or other vulnerable people?** Take extra care with photographs, recordings and names.

The framework cannot answer these for you. It can only avoid getting in the way of your answers.

## Speakers and cultural knowledge

The speaker-visit template is a planning aid. It does not replace local protocol for inviting, honouring and compensating elders, knowledge keepers and speakers. Follow the customs of your community and ask your own people how this should be done.

## Responsible tool use

If you use AI tools, including language models, with community material, consider where that material is sent and stored. Many online tools transmit text and audio to third-party servers and may keep or learn from it, depending on their terms. Check this before using any such tool with community content. Prefer tools you can run locally for sensitive material, and be cautious about machine-generated language content, which can contain errors that a fluent speaker would not make.

## Contributions and removal

Feedback on how to improve these protocols is welcome from community members, language workers and data governance practitioners. If anyone believes material in this repository should not be here, please open an issue or contact the maintainer, and it will be reviewed and removed promptly if appropriate.

## About this document

This document was drafted by a non-specialist and has not yet been reviewed by Indigenous data governance experts or by any community. Treat it as a starting point. Review by the pilot communities, and later by an advisory group if one forms, is a priority before the project is promoted more widely.
