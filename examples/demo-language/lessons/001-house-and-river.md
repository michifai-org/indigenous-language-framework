# The house and the river

## Aims
By the end of this lesson, learners can:
- Say that a house or a river is big
- Say the first sounds of the writing system and hear them in words

## 1. Opening: sounds (required)
- Sounds for today's round: the vowels, then m, t, s, r, l
- New sound or contrast: none yet; this is the first full round
- Who leads: teacher says each sound; learners repeat; then learners take turns around the circle

## 2. Review
First lesson. Nothing to review. Greet each other in whatever language learners already share.

## 3. Place-based anchor (required)
A real house (the classroom building) and, in the lesson, a photo or drawing of the nearest river or creek.

Bring: the picture, and ask learners to point to things that are big and small in the room.

## 4. New material
**Words:** `tumo` (house), `sira` (river), `pelu` (big)

**Pattern (g01, naming a quality):** the thing comes first, then the word that describes it. No verb is needed. `tumo pelu` ("the house is big"), `sira pelu` ("the river is big").

**What stays out today:** anything about people seeing or doing things. That is the next lesson.

## 5. Listening
Learners hear the teacher say both sentences, then point to the matching picture.

## 6. Speaking
Whole group: repeat the two sentences. In pairs: one points to a picture, the other says the sentence. Sentences: `s001`, `s002`.

## 7. Reading
Learners read the two sentences from cards, matching each to a picture.

## 8. Writing
Learners copy the three new words, then write one sentence of their own about something big nearby, using a word the teacher gives them.

## 9. Partner task (required)
Pairs choose five things they can see and say, in turn, whether each is big, using the pattern. Log the session on the partner practice sheet.

## 10. Cultural connection (required)
Invented example: a short story about why the river matters to the community, told by a visitor in the next unit. Today, ask learners to think of one thing about a river near them.

Protocol: no real material is used here. In a real lesson, note any permission needed.

## 11. Check
Hold up a picture. Can the learner say it is big, in the language? Listen for the order of the two words.

If many cannot, repeat the pointing game next time before adding anything new.
