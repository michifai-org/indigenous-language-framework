# I see, you see

## Aims
By the end of this lesson, learners can:
- Say what they see, and ask what someone else sees
- Use the pattern from lesson 1 inside the new one

## 1. Opening: sounds (required)
- Today's round: the whole set so far, led by learners
- New sounds: n, p, k (written k in one spelling, c in the other)

## 2. Review
Pairs say `tumo pelu` and `sira pelu` to each other while pointing at pictures (pattern g01).

## 3. Place-based anchor (required)
Something outside the window, or a walk to the edge of the building. Learners point at a house, a river picture or a stone, and say what they see.

## 4. New material
**Words:** `manu` (see), `kisa` (I), `taru` (you)

**Pattern (g02, saying who sees what):** who sees, then what is seen, then the seeing word. `kisa sira manu` ("I see the river").

**What stays out today:** how to ask a question about what someone sees.

## 5. Listening
Learners listen to the teacher say `kisa sira manu` and `taru tumo manu` and point to who is seeing what.

## 6. Speaking
Pairs take turns: one points at something, the other says what *they* see. Sentences `s003`, `s004`.

## 7. Reading
Read the two new sentences, then re-read `tumo pelu` (`s001`) and combine: "I see the house. The house is big."

## 8. Writing
Write two sentences, one using each pattern.

## 9. Partner task (required)
Pairs walk around their own homes or neighbourhoods and tell each other, in the language, what they see and whether it is big. Log it.

## 10. Cultural connection (required)
Invented example: the unit's visitor will talk about the river. Learners prepare one sentence to say to the visitor.

## 11. Check
Pairs can say who sees what in the correct order. Listen for the verb at the end.
